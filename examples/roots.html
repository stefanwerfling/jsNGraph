Where does data visualization come from?

— the emergence of a discipline

Every cross is a person, placed in the year of a landmark contribution. A coloured strand runs from each person down to every discipline they worked in — economists, engineers, physicians and cartographers first, statisticians throughout, then computer scientists, designers and journalists. Bars count the people per discipline; the dotted arcs below join disciplines that share people. Drag the year slider to watch the bars fill up.

Hover a person, a strand, a bar or an arc to light its part of the web; click to pin it. Wheel zooms, drag pans, double-click fits. The small dials show when each discipline joined in: earliest year just left of the top, counter-clockwise to 1900 at the bottom, up the right side to today.
A personal selection from the history of data visualization; years are those of a landmark contribution and rounded where it spanned years, disciplines simplified. The layout idea follows Fabrice Sabatier's poster “Where does data visualization come from?”; data and drawing are our own.
